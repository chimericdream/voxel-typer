# M4-04 Night Defense mode

Status: done. Waiting on the required feel review.

Night Defense is playable. Friendly visitors walk down lanes toward the child's lantern-lit camp,
each carrying a word on a plate. The first letter locks onto a visitor, the last letter sends it
home in a puff of sparkles, and a block flies to the hotbar. A visitor that reaches the camp only
bonks the shield. **Nothing is ever lost**, and every night ends at dawn.

To try it: `npm run dev`, then
`/?profile=meadow-done-miner&screen=play&lesson=birch-08&nosave=1` (or `fresh-sprout` for calm
mode with the keyboard and hands, `fresh-builder` for five lanes and no keyboard). In the game
itself the nights are Birch Forest lesson 8, Sandy Shore lesson 5 and Frosty Peaks lesson 6.

## The flow

1. **Day** (10 s): the camp in daylight, "Night is coming!". Nothing to type. After 1.5 s any key
   moves the day along; that key is never fed to the engine.
2. **Dusk** (4 s): the sky turns, and the banner names the first wave: "3 slimelings are on the
   way!", or "5 friends are on the way!" for a mixed wave.
3. **Night**: three waves for the shipped lessons (2–5 by lesson length). Between waves there is a
   3 s pause with the next wave's announcement.
   - A visitor appears at the far end of its lane with its word on a plate. Typing the word's
     first letter locks it: the plate grows and gets a bright double frame, and a ring shows under
     the visitor. Typed letters step back and are underlined; the next one has a bar under it.
   - Each right key: a chime that climbs through the word, and the visitor squashes a little.
   - The last letter: a 30 ms hit-stop, the `pop` sound, sparkles and chips of its block, "+1",
     and the block hops to the hotbar. The visitor plays `defeat` and is gone when that ends.
   - A wrong key: nothing is lost. A soft thud, the locked plate shakes its head, the pressed key
     is outlined on the keyboard, and "Almost! Try that key again" shows at most once in 5 s. No
     burst, no shake, no hit-stop.
   - A visitor at the camp: it bonks the shield (the soft `shield` sound, a ripple, one segment
     off the bar), hops back and poofs. At zero the banner says "The shield needs a rest! Keep
     going!" once, and later visitors bounce off the same way. There is no game over.
4. **Dawn** (4.5 s): the sky brightens, "The sun is up! The camp is safe!", the shield grows back,
   the loot lands. Then Results, with two extra lines: "12 friends sent home" and "Best streak: 23
   keys in a row".

**Calm mode** (on by default for Sprouts): visitors stop at a waiting line (a row of flowers,
85 % of the way) and queue there until typed. Nobody new sets out while four are on the field. No
visitor ever reaches the camp, and no clock shows in any mode: a night has no timer at all.

## Decisions

- **The words are on the plates, so the big word display stays empty.** The HUD's top word plate
  would show the locked word twice and move the stage each time it came and went. The locked plate
  is 1.2× bigger and framed instead, and each plate has a spoken name ("Type: frost").
- **The field is framed in the space the HUD leaves** (`fitLaneView`, tested for all three bands).
  A Sprout's keyboard and hands take the lower half of 1280×720, so the open stage is about
  250 px tall, less the banner's room. A flat stage gets a low camera (12°), which folds the lanes
  toward the horizon and keeps the mobs big; a tall one gets 36°. Lanes are 13 blocks long and
  mobs are drawn at 2.2×, so in every band's layout a mob is at least 44 px tall at the camp and
  30 px at the far end (pinned by a test). The picture is slid with a camera view offset, the way
  Mine does it.
- **The camp glows at night.** A warm directional light from the camera's side, off by day and
  full at night, lights the visitors' faces. The night's own light comes from behind them, and
  without it they were dark shapes on a dark lawn. It costs no draw call.
- **Fewer lanes stand further apart** (7 blocks for 3 lanes, 6 for 4, 5 for 5), so a Sprout's
  field and its plates use the width of the screen.
- **Keys with nothing to type are dropped, not counted:** during the day, dusk and dawn, between
  waves, and whenever no visitor is on the field. Under `lockOn` each would be a wrong key.
- **A habit space is dropped** with Mine's own `isStraySpace` (decision 90).
- **Policy `stay` for every band.** A slip never moves the cursor, and Backspace lets go of a
  word. Builder's usual `skip` would leave wrong letters on a moving target.
- **`lockOn` priority is spawn order.** `Target.priority` can't change after `addTargets`, and the
  director now brings mobs to the base in spawn order, so the earliest spawn is the closest in
  time. The director also keeps first letters apart.
- **Stars: accuracy, and most visitors sent home.** `computeStars` with the band table, then
  `nightStars`: the 2nd star needs half the visitors sent home and the 3rd four fifths. Without it
  a child who typed one word well and let the rest walk in, or typed nothing, got three stars.
  The first star is for seeing the night through, always. See Questions 2.
- **Builder's speed star uses the words themselves** (`lockedWpm`: key gaps inside locked words).
  A night's own net WPM also counts the time spent waiting for visitors. Only Builder with a
  `wpmRelative` target is asked for speed.
- **The night's length is a number of waves** from the lesson's length (about one wave per 16–36
  chars by band, or per 30–40 s). The director sizes each wave by time.
- **A key shortens the day.** Ten seconds of watching on every replay is long for a child, so
  after 1.5 s a key leaves half a second.
- **Calm mode holds new visitors while four are out.** Otherwise a slow child had ten queued.
- **The dawn's wait is not typing time.** The session ends when the last wave does.
- **Sparkles are lantern-block chips plus chips of the block dropped.** `FxApi.burst` takes a
  block id; there is no sparkle primitive, and none was added.
- **No white hit flash.** A tint can only darken (M4-01), so a right key is the `hit` squash, the
  chime and the plate's mark. The shield's ripple is the only flash, and it goes through
  `allowFlash()` and is off under reduced motion.
- **Reduced motion:** looping anims hold still (the model runtime), no plate shake, no ring pulse,
  no hop on a bonk, no shield ripple, loot lands at once, and dawn is 2.5 s. `defeat` still plays,
  because the game waits on its end.
- **The Lantern Fish's lantern doesn't glow** (M4-03 note N4). The camp's lanterns are the lights.

`camera.setTarget`, `camera.moveTo`), which Mine's handoff already proposed as additive changes.

1. **Which lessons are nights? (orchestrator, M5-05)** I switched `birch-08`, `shore-05` and
   `frosty-06`. The Meadow has none, so a child still in the Meadow never sees a night; a
   Slimeling night with the ten home-row keys works (the director falls back to the lesson's
   words), and `meadow-12` would be the natural one. I left it for M5-05, whose card says "from
   Birch onward".
2. **`nightStars` (reviewer, pedagogy).** Is "half sent home for the 2nd star, four fifths for the
   3rd" the right gate? The alternative is to count missed words as missed keys in the accuracy.
3. **`lockOn` priority can't follow the mobs (contracts §3.2).** Spawn order is right today. If a
   later mode needs a priority that changes, `TypingSession` needs a `setPriority(id, n)`.
4. **Results lines (orchestrator).** `Results.tsx` is M2-11's file. The hook is small and general;
   say so if it should move or change shape.

5. **A Sprout's stage (feel review, owner).** The keyboard and hands leave Defense about a third
   of the screen. Options, none of them mine to take: hide the hands (not the keyboard) during a
   night; a smaller keyboard in Defense; or accept it. Learning comes before polish, so I kept the
   full keyboard and hands and made the field as big as the space allows.
