### The feel, in words

- **A correct key:** the pickaxe chops (120 ms, down fast and back slower), the block gives a little (it sinks back 5 cm and squashes 3 % for 110 ms), a crack appears or grows, three small chips puff out, and two sounds play: the chip, higher with each crack stage, and a quiet chime that climbs the ladder through the word.
- **The last key:** a 40 ms hit-stop, then the block is gone and 10–30 chips burst out and bounce on the grass. The camera shakes a little (more on a long streak, capped). "+1" floats up, a pixel icon of the block hops in an arc to its hotbar slot and lands with a soft pop, and the slot keeps it with a count. After a 150 ms beat the next block drops in from above with a small overshoot.
- **A wrong key:** the block shakes its head (a spring: about 14° at most, settled in half a second), one soft low thud plays, the pressed key gets a dashed outline on the keyboard, and "Almost! Try that key again" shows for 2.6 s, at most once in 5 s. Nothing is lost: same block, same cracks, same progress. No burst, no shake, no hit-stop.
- **An ore block:** after 10 keys in a row (Sprout; 15 otherwise) the next block is an ore with a slow amber glow, a star chime and "Wow! A shiny block!". It drops two items. A slip before it arrives doesn't take it away.
- **At rest:** the block bobs 6 cm and sways 9° slowly. Under reduced motion and `?freeze` it is still.
- **The end:** the last block gets its full burst and its loot lands, then Results comes up 0.9 s later.

## Decisions

- **The block sits where the HUD leaves room, not in the middle of the screen.** With the real HUD (M2-14), a Sprout's keyboard and hands fill the lower half of a 1280×720 screen and the word is top center, so the open stage in the middle is about 100 px tall. `stageFrame` (pure, tested) picks the spot:
  1. no keyboard, or a tall enough stage (a big screen): the middle, under the word;
  2. otherwise the free column right of the keyboard, in its upper part, with the arm under it. This holds for any word length;
  3. if that column is too narrow too: the middle, as big as fits.
     The mode measures the HUD at the start, on four early frames and on resize, never per frame. The view moves with a camera view offset (the picture slides, its perspective doesn't change), and the block scales to 96–220 px. **For the feel review:** this puts a Sprout's block at the right edge, away from the word and the keys. The alternative is a HUD change (a smaller keyboard or a taller stage), which is M2-14's call.
- **Letter on the block uses `--font-body`** (Atkinson), per M2-08 and the orchestrator, on a dark plate so it reads on any block. It is repainted once the web font has loaded.
- **Float text is the count only ("+1"), on a break only.** A block name next to the right edge was clipped, and the icon that flies to the hotbar already says what it is. It uses the label's default plate colors (`plateText` on `plate`, a checked pair).
- **Mine dirt, get dirt.** An ordinary target shows a `common` Meadow block picked by the table's weights, and a `common` drop becomes that block. Better drops stay as rolled: they are finds from inside. The odds don't change, because both picks use the same weights.
- **Seams.** The same block kind shows for 3–5 letters or 2–3 words in a row, so stacks grow in the hotbar and the stage isn't a new block on every key.
- **Ore veins.** Earned at every multiple of the interval; shown as the next block; kept if the child slips first; one per block even when a long word passes a mark. The ore shown follows the loot thresholds: char rock, then copper at the `rare` streak, then meadowstone (rose) at the `epic` streak.
- **Chime:** climbs with the letters of a word. On single letters it climbs with the streak and starts over every 10. A key that breaks the block plays the break sound instead of the chip.
- **Wrong-key gate** (`feedWrong`): the same key again within 400 ms is one held key (one cue for the whole hold), and cues are at least 350 ms apart. Input already drops `e.repeat`; this also covers keyboards that don't flag repeats. Every wrong press still goes to `reportWrongKey`, as M2-14 asks.
- **Under `skip` (Builder)** a wrong key moves the cursor, so the crack follows it, but there is no swing, chip, chime or puff for it.
- **TTS:** for Sprout with TTS on, each new target is spoken once when it appears, letters included. `app:hearAgain` speaks the current target for any band; the shell gates speech by the setting. `speak` is never retried.
- **Keys wait for the world.** Nothing is typed until the meadow is loaded and the first block is up (a few hundred ms), and the HUD word is empty until then. If the world fails to load, the lesson still starts.
- **The meadow is the same for one player** (seeded from the profile id), so it is their place.
- **Break reminder on the last block (M2-10 N5):** the break overlay shows; the outro and Results wait behind it; resuming resets the reminder and goes on to Results. Tested through the real play host.
- **Timed lessons** (`length.seconds`; none in M2): timed from the first key, the timer is sent only for Miner and Builder outside calm mode, and in calm mode the lesson becomes the same amount of text with no clock.
- **`allowedKeysFor` is still a stub**, so `logic.ts` has `allowedKeys` with the same rule. Switch to the real one when M3-01 lands.
- **The arm and pickaxe** are 11 boxes in M1-14 palette colors: an oak handle, a stepped stone head bound with copper, a plain glove and a blue sleeve. No avatar skin, so it fits every player.

## Contract change request / Questions

None blocking. Mine works through feature checks today; these additive changes would make it clean:

- **`SceneHandle.atlas(): Atlas | null`** and **`CameraRig.setTarget(at)`** (§8). Mine needs the atlas to texture its block and the camera target to frame it. Both exist on M2-01's `SceneCore`; the contract handle doesn't have them, so `mine.ts` narrows with type guards and runs without a 3D stage when they're missing.
- **`ModeContext.frozen: boolean`** (§7) instead of `params.frozen`.
- **A "world ready" signal** (§7 / §13): `ModeInstance.start()` is synchronous, so `__vt.ready()` resolves before the diorama is loaded. A `ready(): Promise<void>` on the instance would let the host hold `loading` until the stage is up.
