# M2-06 Audio synth and SFX

Status: done

## What changed

- `src/audio/engine.ts`: `createAudio(options)` implements `AudioApi` (contracts §12). It makes one
  `AudioContext` on the first `unlock()`, with `music`, `sfx` and `voice` buses → one
  `DynamicsCompressor` → `master` gain → destination. Each `sfx()` call is one voice. Voices are
  capped at 12, and a new voice steals the oldest with a 20 ms fade. `setVolume` and
  `setSpeechEnabled` are here, plus the extras listed below.
- `src/audio/recipes.ts`: every SFX as plain data (layers of tone or noise → optional biquad →
  envelope), with a timbre per material for `break`, `chip` and `place`, `chimeFrequency` (the
  pentatonic ladder) and `MATERIAL_LIST`. No DOM and no clock; randomness (±2% detune) is passed in.
- `src/audio/preview.ts`: seeded white and brown noise (`makeNoise`), the fixed noise start
  offsets (`NOISE_SLOTS` = 16, `noiseOffset`) and an offline renderer in plain TypeScript
  (`renderRecipe`, `worstPeak`, using the Web Audio spec's biquad formulas). Tests and the lab use
  it to measure each sound's worst-case peak without a browser.
- `src/audio/speech.ts`: `createSpeaker` speaks through `speechSynthesis` at rate 0.85, with
  **only on-device** (`localService`) English voices. It never uses a remote voice or the default
  voice.
- `src/audio/graph.ts`: the small slice of Web Audio the engine uses, as structural types. A real
  `AudioContext` fits them, and so does the test fake.
- `src/audio/index.ts`: exports `createAudio`, `MAX_VOICES`, `volumeToGain`, `chimeFrequency`,
  `MATERIAL_LIST`, `recipeFor`, `SPEECH_RATE` and the types. `src/audio/types.ts` is unchanged.
- `audio-lab.html` + `src/dev/audio-lab.ts`: a developer page served by `npm run dev` at
  `/audio-lab.html` (Vite serves root HTML files in dev without config). It is not in `dist/`. It
  has a button for every SFX, and for every material × {break, chip stages 0–9, place, "mine a
  5-letter word"}. It also has the chime ladder, a "20 chimes at once" voice-cap test, volume
  sliders, a speech box, and an inspector that shows the clicked recipe's layers, its waveform and
  its worst-case peak in dBFS, next to a peak table.
- `tests/unit/dom/audio.test.ts`: 37 tests against a hand-rolled fake `AudioContext` that records
  the node graph and every `AudioParam` call.

### Node graphs and peaks (for the review)

Each sound is one voice: `layers… → voice gain (opts.gain, default 1) → sfx bus (sfx²) →
compressor (−18 dB threshold, 12 dB knee, 4:1, 3 ms attack, 250 ms release) → master gain
(master²) → speakers`. Each layer is `source → [biquad] → envelope gain`. The envelope rises
linearly from 0 to `pk` in `a` ms, holds for `h`, then falls exponentially to −80 dB over `d` ms.
`+N ms` is the layer's start offset. Lowpass/highpass `Q` is in dB (Web Audio). Q0 is a linear Q
of 1, which has a small bump of about 1 dB near the cutoff (−3 dB would be Butterworth-flat);
that is negligible here.

**Level after the buses and the compressor (review N1).** At default settings (master 0.8,
sfx 0.8), the bus gains are 0.64 × 0.64 ≈ 0.41 (−7.8 dB). A Web Audio `DynamicsCompressorNode`
also always adds automatic makeup gain of `(1 / curve(1))^0.6`, which cannot be turned off. For
our settings (−18 dB threshold, 12 dB knee, 4:1), I computed the spec's static curve (the
exponential knee, with `k` solved so the slope at the knee end is 1/4). It gives `curve(1)` =
−9.2 dB, so the makeup is about **+5.5 dB**. The review estimated +8.1 dB; either way the
direction and order of magnitude are the same. Sounds below threshold (all of them, at default
volumes) come out about −7.8 + 5.5 ≈ **−2.3 dB** relative to the peaks below. For example, a
stone break peaks near −12.7 dBFS and the thud near −19.7 dBFS. With every slider at 1, the
offset is about +5.5 dB, and pile-ups above −18 dBFS are compressed 4:1. I left the compressor as
it is and documented this, rather than trimming by a number that depends on each browser's makeup
implementation.

Noise layers through a narrow bandpass sound much quieter than their `pk` (the wood knock's
Q10 band passes about 1/20 of white noise's energy), so the measured peak is what counts. Brown
noise is a leaky integrator, so its level varies a lot from slice to slice. That variation caused
the B1 overshoot. To fit the worst slice, I lowered the brown and white noise levels on dirt,
stone, sand, snow, place and cart, and the chip noise on every material:

The tests enforce these at the same worst case: every sound ≤ 0.33 (−9.6 dBFS), every chip ≤ 0.14
(−17.1 dBFS), and thud ≤ 0.15. The highest are the dirt break at 0.313 and the stone chip at 0.133.

**Why the thud is gentle.** It is a low sine (165→110 Hz) with a soft triangle a fourth above
(220→165 Hz), and the triangle's overtones are lowpassed away. Both slide down a little, like a
soft "bmp". The 12–15 ms attack has no click, and there is no noise, no buzz, no dissonant
interval and nothing above about 500 Hz. Peak −17.4 dBFS, about the chime's level. Because the
energy sits around 110–165 Hz, it sounds quieter than the chime and much quieter than a break.
The lab has "chip, chip, thud, chip" and "thud × 4" buttons to hear it in context.

## Decisions

- **The context is created inside the first `unlock()`, not at startup.** `sfx()` before unlock
  is a silent no-op, so nothing makes an `AudioContext` before a gesture (Chrome warns about that)
  and calls never queue up to burst out at once.
- **Slider-to-gain curve is `gain = v²`** for all buses and for speech volume, so the slider feels
  even across its range. `setVolume` clamps to 0–1 and glides (15 ms time constant) to avoid
  zipper noise. Volume 0 is mute; `sfx` then makes no nodes at all.
- **Compressor before the master gain**, so it acts the same at any master volume and catches
  pile-ups (up to 12 voices).
- **`opts.gain` is clamped to 0–1** (a multiplier on the voice), so no caller can push an effect
  above its designed level.
- **`chip`'s `pitchStep` is the crack stage 0–9**, rising 1.5 semitones a stage (just over an
  octave in total). `chime`'s `pitchStep` is floored and clamped to 0–10 (C5 to C7). Negative and
  NaN become 0.
- **Default material is `stone`** for `break`, `chip` and `place` when `material` is missing.
- **Seeds**: noise buffers use `createRng(seed).fork("audio:noise")`, and the ±2% detune and the
  noise slot use `fork("audio:vary")`, with `DEFAULT_AUDIO_SEED = 0x766f78`. Each noise buffer is
  one second long.
- **Noise offsets are 16 fixed slots (review B1, option a).** Each voice picks one slot, and all
  its noise layers start at `noiseOffset(slot, layerLength)`, evenly spaced through the buffer.
  That still gives enough variety that repeated chips don't sound copy-pasted, and the tests can
  measure every slice the engine plays. I chose this over normalizing the brown noise per block,
  because gain steps between blocks would add clicks.
- **Speech is on-device only (review B2, child privacy).** `pickVoice` accepts a voice only if
  `localService === true` and its `lang` is `en`, `en-*` or `en_*` (Android uses `_`). Remote
  voices are never used, even when they come first in the list. There is no fallback to the
  browser's default voice. With no local English voice (an empty or still-loading list, or
  remote voices only), `speak` does nothing. The latest word asked for is kept, then spoken on the
  next `voiceschanged` if a local English voice has appeared, and dropped otherwise. Speech uses
  rate 0.85 and calls `cancel()` before each utterance, so "hear again" never queues. Blank text
  is a no-op, and `setSpeechEnabled(false)` also drops a waiting word. Allowing remote voices
  would need the owner's decision; I have not asked for one.
- **Voice stealing**: oldest first; a 20 ms linear fade, then its sources stop at +30 ms. Stolen
  voices are not counted toward the cap while they fade.
- **Reduced motion** has no audio side (no setting in `contracts.md` ties sound to it), so the
  engine ignores it. Audio respects `Settings.volume` (through `setVolume`) and `Settings.tts`
  (through `setSpeechEnabled`).
- **No `vite.config.ts` change.** Vite's dev server serves `audio-lab.html` from the root without
  one, and no e2e needs the page, so it stays out of both the test and production builds.
- `music` is a set of no-op stubs. M6-01 owns `src/audio/music/*.ts`.

## Notes for downstream tasks

- **API beyond the contract** (`AudioEngine extends AudioApi`): `setSpeechEnabled(on)` (mirror
  `Settings.tts`), `busNode(bus)` (a bus's `GainNode`, null before unlock) and `activeVoices()`.
  `createAudio({ createContext, speech, seed, maxVoices })` lets tests inject fakes.
- **M2-10 (app shell)**: make one engine with `createAudio()`. Call `unlock()` from the first
  keydown or pointerdown (the splash's "Press any key"); calling it on every gesture is safe and
  also resumes a context the browser suspended. On profile load and on settings change, call
  `setVolume(bus, settings.volume[bus])` for all four buses and
  `setSpeechEnabled(settings.tts)`, before anything can call `speak` (the engine starts with
  speech on).
- **M2-13 (mine mode)**: `sfx("chip", { material, pitchStep: crackStage })` (0–9),
  `sfx("chime", { pitchStep: streakInWord })`, `sfx("break", { material })`, `sfx("thud")` on a
  wrong key. Each block's `material` comes from the registry (M2-04).
- **M6-01 (music)**: connect your output to `busNode("music")` after `unlock()`. It already runs
  through the master compressor. Fill in the `music` stubs in `engine.ts` (or have the engine
  delegate to your module); that file is in `src/audio/*.ts`, so coordinate with the orchestrator.
- `src/audio/preview.ts` can render any recipe offline. It is a cheap way to unit-test loudness
  if you add or change sounds. Keep the `recipes` tests' limits.
- The loudness tests measure every noise slot × 3 seeds × 3 detunes. That takes about 3 s locally,
  and the test has a 30 s timeout. If you change `NOISE_SLOTS`, `noiseOffset` or the noise
  generator, the budgets must still pass.
- On Chrome for Linux without speech-dispatcher, and on some Chromebooks, the only English voices
  are remote, so `speak` (Sprout "hear again") is silent there. That is on purpose. M2-10 or
  M2-13 may want to hide "hear again" when there is no local voice; I can add a
  `hasLocalVoice()` helper on request.

### Review notes left for later (non-blocking)

- **N2**: if the first `unlock()` runs without user activation, Chrome can leave `resume()`
  pending forever. Every later `unlock()` then returns that same promise without calling
  `resume()` again. M2-10 must call `unlock()` only from a real gesture (keydown or pointerdown).
  A later fix: call `ctx.resume()` on every `unlock()` while `state !== "running"`.
- **N3**: `speak()` isn't gated on `unlock()`. Browsers block speech before user activation
  anyway, so this is harmless.
- **N4**: `speechOn` defaults to `true`, but some bands default to `tts: false`. **M2-10 must call
  `setSpeechEnabled(settings.tts)` before any `speak`.** Defaulting the engine to off is the
  alternative.
- **N5**: the thud's energy is at 110–165 Hz, which tablet and laptop speakers barely reproduce,
  so on those it may be close to silent (the safe failure mode). Check it on a tablet; if it
  vanishes, raise the triangle partial slightly rather than the level.
- **N6**: lowpass Q0 is not perfectly flat (about +1 dB at the cutoff); the wording above is
  corrected.
